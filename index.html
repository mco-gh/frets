          About Frets

          What it is

          Frets is a free flashcard app for learning the notes on the guitar neck. Each card shows an electric guitar fretboard in standard tuning (E A D G B E) with one spot highlighted. Name the note, then flip the card to check: the back names it and rings every other place on the neck with the same note, with solid rings where it’s the very same pitch.

          What you can do

          • Learn a few new notes each day: 10 by default, adjustable in Settings.
          • Tap the note on the keys under the card, or type it (A–G; hold Shift for a sharp). Or just flip the card and be honest with yourself.
          • Choose how many frets your guitar has (22 by default, up to 24), whether to include sharps and flats, and which strings to study.
          • Review each note just before you’re likely to forget it. This is called spaced repetition: notes you know well come back less and less often.
          • Rate each card Again, Hard, Good or Easy. Your answer decides when you’ll see it next.
          • See the whole neck at once in Neck, colored by how well you know each note, and pick out every C, or every F♯.
          • Follow your streak, mastery and upcoming reviews in Progress.
          • Use it offline, or install it on your phone or computer. There’s no account: your progress stays in this browser, and you can export a backup from Settings.

          A few tips

          • The 12th fret is the open string an octave up, so the pattern repeats from there.
          • Between E and F, and between B and C, there is only one fret. Every other pair of neighboring natural notes is two frets apart.
          • The 5th fret of each string is the next string’s open note, except on the G string, where it’s the 4th fret.

          Credits
